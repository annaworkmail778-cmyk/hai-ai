## 3. Conditional finalists: detailed analysis

### Finalist A: AI Sales-Order Intake ("Order-to-ERP"). Score 7.5 → could reach 9 with 2 paid design partners

**A. Problem**
- Distributors and manufacturers receive purchase orders as PDFs, emails, spreadsheets, photos, and (in our region) WhatsApp or Telegram messages.
- Customer-service staff retype them into the ERP or accounting system. That is slow, error-prone (wrong SKU or quantity → returns, credits, unhappy customers) and scales only with headcount.
- **Who feels it:** order desk / customer service, the operations manager, and the owner or COO.
- **Why existing solutions fail mid-market:**
  - Enterprise tools (Conexiom) are SAP-centric and quote-based [S1].
  - Wholesale platforms (WizCommerce, Orderlion) ask customers to change ordering behaviour or adopt a new storefront [S2].
  - Smaller firms on QuickBooks/Xero/NetSuite, Odoo, 1C or Armsoft have nothing that fits.

**B. Ideal customer profile [ESTIMATE/HYPOTHESIS]**

| | |
|---|---|
| Who | B2B distributor or manufacturer, 20–300 employees, ≥ 30 orders/day, ≥ 1 full-time order-entry person |
| Geography | (1) Armenia/Georgia: fast, in-person access. (2) UK/EU/US mid-market via LinkedIn/email: higher ticket |
| Buyer | Owner/COO (SMB) or VP Operations / Customer Service Director (mid-market) |
| Budget authority | Owner: unilateral up to a few thousand dollars [HYPOTHESIS] |

**C. Financial impact.** The formula is shown so the buyer's own numbers can be plugged in.

> Annual cost of manual entry = orders/day × minutes/order ÷ 60 × loaded hourly cost × working days
> + error rate × orders × cost per error

**US/UK example [ESTIMATE]:**
- Assumptions: 60 orders/day × 8 min ÷ 60 × $28/hour loaded × 250 days ≈ **$56,000/year** in entry labour.
- Plus errors: 1.5% × 15,000 orders × $50 ≈ **$11,250/year**.
- **Total ≈ $67,000/year.**
- **If 70% of orders become one-click review:** savings ≈ $39,000 labour + ~$7,000 errors ≈ **$46,000/year**.
- **Price:** $6,000 setup + $1,200/month → year-1 cost $20,400 → **payback ≈ 5 months, year-1 ROI ≈ 125%** [ESTIMATE].

**Armenia example [ESTIMATE]:**
- Assumptions: 40 orders/day × 6 min × 2,500 AMD/hour loaded × 250 days ≈ 2.5M AMD/year (~$6.4k).
- **This justifies only about 600k–900k AMD setup + 80–120k AMD/month.** Local willingness to pay is structurally lower, so local deals are for speed and references and international deals are for margin.

Vendor claims for manual order cost range from €3–9 to $30–80 per order and are divergent and self-interested [S2]. **Always measure the buyer's own baseline.**

**D. Solution**
- **Intake:** a dedicated mailbox (Gmail/Microsoft Graph), an official WhatsApp Cloud API number or Telegram bot, and file upload.
- **Extraction:** an LLM with structured output plus OCR for scans → customer, PO number, lines, quantities, dates, delivery address.
- **Catalogue matching:** fuzzy plus learned aliases per customer ("their SKU" → "our SKU"), using price lists and units of measure.
- **Validation rules:** credit hold, minimum order, price variance, missing fields.
- **Review queue:** a simple web UI (Next.js + Supabase). Staff approve or correct, and corrections feed the alias table.
- **Push to ERP:** API for NetSuite, Odoo, QuickBooks/Xero or Business Central; CSV/XML import for 1C or Armsoft.
- **Confirmation:** an order acknowledgement to the customer (approved template).
- **Metrics:** touchless rate, time per order, error rate.
- **Human involvement:** always a human approves at the start; auto-post only above a confidence threshold, agreed with the client.
- **Complexity:** medium. **Delivery timeline:** 3–6 weeks per client.

**E. Pricing [ESTIMATE, anchored to S1/S2]**
| Market | Paid discovery (2 weeks, accuracy test on 200 real orders) | Setup | Monthly | Performance option |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| UK/EU/US | $1,000–1,500 (credited) | $4,000–10,000 | $800–2,000 (volume tiers) | per-order fee above the tier |
| Armenia/Georgia | 120–200k AMD | 600k–1.2M AMD | 80–150k AMD | — |

- **Gross margin:** about 75–85% (LLM cost per order is cents; **measure it**).
- **Upsells:** receivables (Finalist B), quote automation, dashboards.

**F. Competition**

| Player | Model / price | Weakness for our segment |
|---|---|---|
| Conexiom | Enterprise; $10k/yr on the SAP Store [S1] | SAP-centric, quote-based |
| OrderSync | $1k+/month + per order [S2] | Price floor too high for small distributors |
| Orderlion / WizCommerce / eGrow | Ordering platforms with AI inbox [S2] | Ask the customer's buyers to change channel, or are vertical-specific |
| Offshore data entry | Per-hour or per-document [S4] | Error-prone, slow at peaks |
| Do nothing (hire another clerk) | Salary | Scales linearly |

**Our edge:**
- We fit the existing ERP and channels (including WhatsApp, Telegram, Russian and Armenian).
- Implementation is done for them.
- The price sits between offshore staff and enterprise software.

**G. Sales**
- **First 100 prospects:**
  - 40 local (wholesale markets, supermarket/HoReCa supplier lists, building-material distributors; manual and sourced);
  - 60 UK/EU/US (LinkedIn titles: "Customer Service Manager", "Order Desk", "Operations Director" at distributors with 20–300 staff; industry association member lists).
- **Approach:** in person locally; personal LinkedIn/email internationally.
- **Opening:**
  > "How many orders a day does your team retype from email or PDF? Send 20 anonymised ones, and we'll show you the extracted, ERP-ready orders and the accuracy within 48 hours, free."
- **Demo:** their own sample (anonymised) → a structured table → mock ERP import, with accuracy shown honestly.
- **Objections:**
  - "Our ERP is old" → CSV/XML import.
  - "Accuracy?" → human review + measured rates.
  - "Data security" → NDA, data processing agreement, EU hosting option, least access.
- **Close:** a paid discovery with a written success criterion (e.g., ≥ 85% lines correct without edits, ≥ 50% time saved), then setup.

**H. Delivery and scale**
- **Standardised:** intake, extraction schema, review UI, metrics.
- **Custom:** ERP adapter, catalogue rules.
- **Capacity:** with two people, 2 implementations in parallel.
- **Beyond 10 clients:** an adapter library (NetSuite, Odoo, QuickBooks, 1C) + a self-serve alias trainer, which becomes a SaaS core.

**What would make it 9/10:**
- 2 paid discoveries converted to setups;
- measured ≥ 85% touchless accuracy;
- ≥ 50% time reduction on real data.

### Finalist B: Receivables Autopilot. Score 7.35 (fastest cash)

**A. Problem**
- Late B2B payments strain cash flow.
- Chasing is manual and awkward, often done by the owner or accountant.

**B. ICP**
- B2B SMEs with ≥ 30 credit customers: distributors (the same list as A), logistics, printing, B2B services.
- Armenia/Georgia first; UK SMEs later.

**C. Impact [ESTIMATE]**
> Value ≈ overdue balance × cost of capital × days accelerated ÷ 365 + reduced bad debt + staff hours

**Example:**
- 40M AMD overdue × 18%/year × 20 days faster ÷ 365 ≈ **395k AMD/year** in financing value.
- Plus fewer write-offs (e.g., 1% of 40M = 400k).
- Plus ~4 hours/week of staff time.
- **Total ≈ 1M+ AMD/year** → justifies **300–500k AMD setup + 50–100k/month**.

**D. Solution**
- **Data:** aging export from the accounting system (and, where possible, the SRC e-invoicing data [S19]).
- **Process:** reminder sequences (email, Telegram, WhatsApp) in hy/ru/en; promise-to-pay capture; escalation tasks; a weekly cash forecast.
- **Build:** 4–6 days.

**E. Pricing**
- Local: 300–500k AMD setup + 50–100k AMD/month.
- UK: £1,500–3,000 setup + £300–600/month [ESTIMATE, below Chaser/Upflow total cost].

**F. Competition**
- Chaser (~$259/month+) and Upflow (quote, ~$15k average) [S3]; accounting-system reminders.
- **Our edge:** local language and tone, fitting local accounting systems, done-for-you.

**G. Sales**

- **Opening:**

### Finalist C: Freight broker/forwarder "Digital Back Office". Score 7.45 (high ticket)

**A. Problem**
- Brokers and forwarders handle floods of emailed quote requests, load tenders, shipment documents and status checks.
- Margins are thin, so they offshore staff or overwork their team [S4].
- Funded startups target exactly this [S5], which is strong evidence of demand, but also of competition.

**B. ICP**
- US/UK freight brokers and forwarders with 10–100 staff using a mid-market TMS.
- Decision maker: the owner or VP Operations.
- **US broker authority data is public (FMCSA)**, so list building is legitimate.

**C. Impact [ESTIMATE]**
- Example: 3 back-office staff × $4,400/month US cost (midpoint of S4's $3.8–5k) ≈ **$158k/year**.
- Automating 50% of transactional tasks (rate requests, load entry, check calls) → about **$79k/year** of capacity.
- Price: $8,000 setup + $2,000/month → year-1 $32k → **payback ≈ 5 months**.
- **Against an offshore baseline** ($1.4–1.8k per person per month [S4]), the savings are much smaller. **Position as capacity and speed** (Vooma reports about 5 minutes saved per quote [S5]), **not only headcount**.

**D. Solution**
- Inbox AI that classifies email (quote request, tender, POD, status).
- Extraction into the TMS via API, or browser automation where permitted.
- Draft quote responses from the broker's pricing rules.
- Automated status updates from carrier and tracking feeds.
- A human-in-the-loop review queue.

**E. Pricing:** $3,000–10,000 setup + $1,000–3,000/month, or a per-load fee [ESTIMATE; no public competitor price found S5].

**F. Competition:**
- Vooma, Parade (funded) [S5];
- TMS-native AI;
- offshore BPO [S4].
- **Our edge:** custom fit for small brokers on varied TMSs; lower cost than BPO plus software combined.

**G. Sales**
- **Who:** FMCSA-listed brokers + LinkedIn.
- **Opening:** "We cut quote-response time from hours to minutes without changing your TMS. Free analysis of 50 of your quote emails."
- **Cycle:** 4–10 weeks.

**H. Risks:**
- TMS API access;
- data security;
- carrier/load fraud context;
- US time zones;
- trust in an offshore vendor.

## 7. Why focus on three, not ten
C1 and C3 sell to **the same person** (the distributor owner/COO) from **one prospect list**, using **the same skills** (document/data extraction, accounting/ERP data, messaging). C2 reuses C1's engine (email → structured record → system of record) in a higher-ticket vertical once C1 has proof. Spreading across chargebacks, dental, legal and hotels would split scarce selling time across unrelated buyers and compliance regimes.

## 8. 14-day validation plan

| Day | Actions | Output |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Build list A: 40 local distributors/manufacturers (sourced). List B: 50 UK/EU/US distributors (LinkedIn, association lists). List C: 30 US freight brokers (FMCSA public data + LinkedIn). Draft NDA + data processing agreement templates (lawyer review requested) | 120 sourced prospects |
| 2–3 | Build a **demo with illustrative data**: 30 messy orders (email, PDF, photo, WhatsApp text) → extraction → catalogue match → review screen → CSV for a mock ERP. Also a receivables demo (aging list → sequence → forecast) | Two working demos |
| 3–7 | Local: 15 in-person visits (owner/COO). International: 50 personal emails/LinkedIn messages offering a **free 48-hour accuracy test on 20 anonymised orders**. Freight: 30 personal messages offering a free analysis of 50 quote emails | ≥ 12 conversations, ≥ 6 sample datasets received |
| 5–10 | Run accuracy tests on real samples. Report results honestly (lines correct, time per order). Present the price and ask for a **paid discovery** (local 120–200k AMD; intl $1,000–1,500) and, for receivables, a **paid 30-day pilot** (200k AMD) | Proposals with deposit invoices |
| 11–14 | Close. Collect deposits. Start the first discovery | Go/no-go |

**Go/no-go thresholds (day 14):**

| Idea | Go if… | Kill or pause if… |

**Rules:**
- "Interesting" counts as zero.
- Sample data received counts as weak validation.
- A paid discovery or pilot counts as strong validation.
- A signed setup with deposit counts as proof.

## 9. Risks that could make this fail
1. **Selling:** two people, no references, and an international trust gap. Mitigation: paid discoveries, measured results, local references first.
2. **Accuracy:** messy documents and Armenian/Russian handwriting in photos [HYPOTHESIS]. Mitigation: a human-in-the-loop review queue; publish measured accuracy.
3. **Integration:** legacy ERPs. Mitigation: CSV/XML import first.
4. **Security and privacy:** customer and order data under GDPR for EU clients. Mitigation: data processing agreement, minimal retention, EU hosting option, access logs.
5. **Competition:** funded vendors (Vooma, Parade, Conexiom, Orderlion) may move down-market. Mitigation: a regional/language niche + done-for-you integration.
6. **Payments:** Stripe isn't available to Armenian merchants; PayPal can't receive commercial payments [prior S6]. Mitigation: invoice + bank transfer / Payoneer for international clients.
